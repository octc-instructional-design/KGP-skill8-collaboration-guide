You Need Less Than You Think

The Short, Honest Version

That reputation comes from one specific thing: the big, multi-week team project where someone vanishes, someone else does everything, and the grade gets messy — usually in an online course where no one can find a time to meet. That version is real. It's also one option at the far end of a wide range, and most courses never need it.

  • You need less than you think. A ten-minute activity legitimately builds this skill. You do not have to assign a project.
  • The parts that burned you live at one end of the scale — the far end — and you can skip it entirely and still meet the requirement.
  • You're not starting from a blank page. Every activity, template, and rubric you need is already here, ready to copy.

One thing to be clear about up front: this is not a competency checklist students get formally checked off on, and it is not a rubric you're required to adopt. It's a resource for isolating the behaviors that matter for Skill 8 and turning the ones relevant to your assignment into an assessment that's yours.

The Range

There are four levels. Everything in this guide is organized around them, and the whole argument of this page is that most courses belong in the first two.

1

Quick

One class session or less. Students contribute and respond — that's it.

~10 min to set up
2

Structured

A few days. Students give each other real feedback, not just post alongside each other.

A few days
3

Coordinated

About a week. Divided roles, and the pieces have to fit together at the end.

~1 week
4

Sustained

Several weeks. A real team project — and yes, this is the one you're picturing.

Multiple weeks

Level 4 is optional. It's there for faculty who want it, in courses where it fits. Unless your course is responsible for 8.3 or 8.4, nothing requires you to go past level 1.

The Smallest Thing That Counts

Level 1 · Works online · ~10 minutes to set up · no grading

Open a Discussion in your Blackboard course, post one question tied to the day's topic, and ask each student to add an idea and reply to one classmate. That's it — no new tool, it's the Discussion board you already have, and every student can reach it on any device, including a Chromebook. You just glance at the thread. That is a complete Skill 8 activity. No teams, no contract, no group grade.

Where Each Worry Actually Lives

Notice that every group-work horror story happens at the heavy end. Small activities can't produce them — there's no time and no cover.

The worryWhere it actually happensSo instead…
A few people do all the workLong multi-week projects (level 4)Use a short activity — there's no time or cover for free-riding
Someone disappears (ghosting)Team projects that run for weeks (level 4)Level 1 and 2 activities are over in a class or two — no one can vanish
I can't grade it fairlyWhen one shared grade is the only gradeSmall activities fold into participation — nothing complicated to score
Online is impossible to coordinateMaking students meet at the same timeLet the activity be a post in a discussion — no meeting required

And if something goes sideways anyway — a student goes quiet, one person takes over — you don't have to improvise. 2.0 | Facilitating Effective Group Work tells you exactly what to do, and when to do nothing.

Ready for You Right Now

  • Run-as-is activities. Open the examples for your level, copy one, and run it — in person or online. (9.0 | Examples)
  • A sizing guide. Not sure which level fits your course? It will tell you — almost always smaller than you think. (4.0 | Choosing Your Approach)
  • What to do while it's running. How much to watch, when to step in, and how to close it out — in person, online, or hybrid. (2.0 | Facilitating Effective Group Work)
  • Ready-made templates. Group contract, Collaboration Profile Survey, peer evaluation form — don't write them, grab them. (7.0 | Group Work Tools)
  • Tools you already have. Blackboard Discussions and Groups for everything online, and Microsoft Teams when a team needs a workspace — no new software to learn. (8.0 | Technology Tools)
  • A rubric you build in minutes. Pick the behaviors you care about; no rubric from scratch. (6.0 | Build Your Rubric)

Your First Move

Pick one class session in the next two weeks and run the Discussion activity above. Ten minutes to set up, nothing to grade — just see how it goes. That single move legitimately builds Essential Skill 8. Everything else in this guide is here for when you want to go a little further, at your pace.

You Have Backup

  • You're not building from scratch. Every activity, template, and rubric here is made to copy and adapt — that's the whole point of this guide.
  • Start with one section of one course. Get it working once, then reuse it every term.
  • Want a second set of eyes? Reach the instructional design team — helping you set this up is exactly what we're here for.
