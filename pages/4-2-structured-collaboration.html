4.2 Structured Collaboration in Action

A protocol and a short exchange that builds Indicator 8.2 — communicating clearly and giving and receiving feedback well — over a class or a few days.

Level 2 · Structured  ·  Builds Indicator 8.2

What a Structured move is: a protocol and a few moving parts, but still short — a class or a few days. Students respond to and build on each other's work, and the first real friction shows up. That is exactly where 8.2 lives: communicating expectations and handling disagreement professionally.

Online and asynchronous is still the case to spell out. The exchange becomes a written record — and in a Structured move, the feedback a student writes is the evidence. This is where Blackboard starts to earn its place: peer review and graded discussions put the exchange on the record so you can assess its quality rather than just confirm it happened.

Assessment fit: grade the quality of the feedback or exchange, not just that it happened. A light individual grade or a short feedback rubric does it. Still no group grade and no contract.

Three Structured Activities

1. Structured Peer Review

Students exchange a piece of individual work and give feedback using a short protocol — two specific, actionable notes each. The feedback they write is the evidence.
Builds 8.2
SettingHow to run it
In personPairs or trios swap drafts. Each gives two notes on a feedback sheet — one thing that's working, one specific fix — then partners discuss for a few minutes.
Online — synchronousPairs in Teams breakout rooms review each other's shared file; notes go in the file comments and the chat; quick debrief in the main room.
Online — asynchronousUse Blackboard's peer review on the assignment, or a Blackboard Discussion where each student posts a draft and replies to two assigned classmates with structured feedback. The written feedback is on the record and gradable for quality.
Try this prompt

Career / first-year: “Exchange draft cover letters for a real local job posting. Give each writer two specific, actionable notes — one thing that's working, one thing to sharpen.”

2. Respond-and-Build Discussion

A threaded exchange on a question where reasonable people disagree. Students must engage a classmate — extending the idea or respectfully pushing back — using sentence stems, so disagreement stays professional.
Builds 8.2
SettingHow to run it
In personRun a structured discussion: before adding a point, each student restates the previous one (“What I hear you saying is…”), then extends or counters it.
Online — synchronousSame protocol live in Teams; require each turn to begin by acknowledging the last speaker before responding.
Online — asynchronousPost the prompt in a Blackboard Discussion. Each student posts a position, then replies to at least one peer using a stem — “Building on…,” “I see it differently because…” The thread shows whether students engaged the disagreement constructively. Set the post deadline a day or two before the reply deadline, or everything lands at once with nothing to respond to.
Try this prompt

Health / general education ethics: “Should a patient always be told everything, even when family asks you not to? Take a position, then respond to a classmate who landed somewhere different.”

3. Critique-the-Model

The class gives structured feedback on an anonymous or instructor-provided sample — safe practice in specific, respectful, actionable feedback before doing it to a classmate.
Builds 8.2
SettingHow to run it
In personShow a sample (a deliberately middling example). In pairs, students produce two concrete improvements using the protocol; share out and compare what people noticed.
Online — synchronousSame, with the sample on a shared screen and quick Teams breakouts to draft improvements.
Online — asynchronousPost the sample in a Blackboard Discussion. Each student adds one specific, actionable improvement, then endorses the strongest suggestion already posted and says why. Fast and low-stakes — it builds the feedback vocabulary before students review each other's real work.
Try this prompt

Trades / health: “Here is a sample job-safety plan (or care-plan note). Add one specific, actionable improvement — name the exact change, not just ‘make it clearer.’”

What Keeps It Structured

  • A protocol and a real exchange, but a short horizon — a class or a few days.
  • Students still work as individuals; what's shared is feedback and response, not a single group deliverable with a shared grade.
  • If you divide a task into parts that depend on each other, you've moved up to Coordinated. If it runs for weeks with a contract and peer evaluations, that's Sustained.

For Faculty

  • Grade the feedback, not the draft. In a Structured move the skill is the quality of the exchange — a short rubric for “specific, respectful, actionable” assesses 8.2 directly.
  • The async record is the evidence. A peer-review thread or discussion shows exactly how each student handled the exchange — you can assess it without having witnessed anything.
  • Sentence stems do a lot of work. Giving students openers for agreeing, extending, and respectfully disagreeing keeps online exchanges professional and on topic.
  • Practice on a model first. Critique-the-Model lowers the stakes and raises the quality before students review each other's real work.
