3.2 8.2  Professional Communication & Feedback

One of four indicators of Essential Skill 8: “Graduates will collaborate with colleagues, become effective team members, and manage conflict.” This one is about communicating clearly, responding to others, and handling disagreement like a professional.

Assessable from level 2  ·  Structured onward (partly at level 1)

Benchmark · Starting Point Milestone 8.2 · End of Two Years of College
Responds promptly to messages and requests for information. Addresses conflicts constructively, communicating expectations and adjusting own behavior based on feedback received.

I Can Statements

  • I can address disagreements or tension within a team in a professional and constructive way.
  • I can communicate my expectations clearly without creating unnecessary conflict.
  • I can receive feedback from teammates and adjust my behavior accordingly.

What It Looks Like

Observable behaviors — things you can point at in a real course. Not a checklist to score, and not a count.

  • Responds to team conflict or disagreement without dismissing it, avoiding it, or escalating it
  • States a concern or expectation directly to the team rather than working around it or bringing it only to the instructor
  • Changes their approach after receiving peer or instructor feedback — and can say what they changed
  • Gives feedback that names something specific and suggests a next step, rather than a general reaction

How to Assess It

Moves that work in any course. Pick the column that matches where your class meets — the content of the disagreement is yours to supply.

In Person

  • Two stars and a step Each student names two specific strengths and one concrete next step on a teammate's work, out loud. You hear the quality of the feedback directly.
  • Friction check Midway, each team names one point of friction and proposes a resolution — two minutes, verbal or on a card. Turns conflict into a scheduled observation point.
  • Build in the disagreement Give the task a genuine either/or. You cannot assess conflict handling in an activity where nobody disagrees.

Online · Asynchronous

  • Peer review with a form Students give written feedback on a teammate's contribution. Assess the feedback itself — is it specific, does it suggest a next step — not whether it was submitted.
  • Revise and report After receiving feedback, each student posts one line on what they changed and why. The cleanest possible evidence of adjusting behavior, and it costs a sentence.
  • Sentence stems Require openers for disagreement — “Building on…,” “I see it differently because…” Structure lowers the temperature without suppressing the disagreement, which matters more in writing than in a room.

Hybrid

  • Disagree in writing, resolve in the room Positions get posted so there's a record to assess; the repair conversation happens live, where it's far faster.
  • Debrief a real thread Pull one exchange from the discussion into class and work it as a group — what landed, what to say differently. Feedback on feedback.

Practical Tips for Faculty

  • Conflict in group work is an assessment opportunity, not just a management problem — building in structured reflection turns it into observable, gradable behavior
  • Don't rescue a team from disagreement too early. While it's about the work, it is the skill. When to step in is covered in 2.0 | Facilitating Effective Group Work.
  • Peer feedback quality is assessable; give students a rubric for giving feedback, not just receiving it
  • In online courses, the written record of team communication makes tone and responsiveness directly visible — and it means you can assess this without having witnessed anything
