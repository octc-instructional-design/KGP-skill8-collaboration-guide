4.3 Coordinated Collaboration in Action

Divided work with real dependencies that builds Indicator 8.3 — identifying tasks and distributing roles by strength — across a class to a week.

Level 3 · Coordinated  ·  Builds Indicator 8.3

What a Coordinated move is: the work is genuinely divided, and the group can't finish without every part — real dependencies between people. But the window is short, a class to about a week, so free-riding has little room to grow. That interdependence is what builds 8.3: breaking a goal into tasks and distributing roles by strength.

This is the first level with real setup. Divided work needs a place to live, so the async case introduces Blackboard Groups — the group container and the graded record in one place. For a one-week task, the team coordinates in a Teams channel and assembles the parts in a shared file there. The tools get heavier here because the work does; that's the trade-off you're choosing.

Assessment fit: a light shared-deliverable grade plus a quick individual accountability piece — each person submits their part or a one-line contribution note. A single peer-check question is optional. This is not the full three-part model in 5.0; that belongs to Sustained.

Two Coordinated Activities

1. Jigsaw

Each member becomes the “expert” on one piece and is responsible for teaching it back. The whole can't come together without every piece — interdependence is built into the structure.
Builds 8.3
SettingHow to run it
In personSplit the topic into parts. “Expert” groups master one part each, then reshuffle into mixed “home” groups where each person teaches their piece and the group assembles the whole.
Online — synchronousRun expert breakout rooms in Teams first, then reshuffle into home rooms where each member teaches their part live.
Online — asynchronousCreate a Blackboard Group set. Each member is assigned a subtopic and posts their “expert” piece to the group's Discussion; the group then assembles the parts into one shared artifact. Each member submits their own piece, so individual accountability is built in rather than negotiated.
Try this prompt

Anatomy & Physiology (or Marketing): “Each member takes one body system (or one of the four Ps) and produces a clear one-paragraph explainer plus one exam-style question. The group combines them into a single study guide.”

2. Divided-Role Mini-Project (one week)

A single short shared deliverable with assigned, interdependent roles and a one-week window. Everyone owns a part, and the deliverable doesn't come together unless the parts do.
Builds 8.3
SettingHow to run it
In personAssign roles in class — researcher, organizer, checker, presenter — work over a few days, then present or submit the shared deliverable.
Online — synchronousA short Teams meeting to assign roles and plan, async work in between, then reconvene to finalize.
Online — asynchronousSet up a Blackboard Group set with a group assignment and due date. Each team coordinates in its Teams channel; each role produces a part; the team coordinator submits the shared deliverable in Blackboard, and each member posts a one-line note on what they contributed.
Try this prompt

Nursing / Business / Trades: “In one week, your team produces a single one-page patient-education sheet (or mini market snapshot, or job-safety plan). Assign roles up front; each person owns a section.”

Notice the resemblance. This is the short, light cousin of the Sustained exemplars in 9.0 | Examples. Stretch it across several weeks, introduce changing requirements, and add a group contract and peer evaluations, and you've moved up to Level 4 · Sustained — same shape, more time and more apparatus.

What Keeps It Coordinated

  • Genuine division of labor, but a short timeline — a class to about a week.
  • Light accountability: each member submits their part or a one-line contribution note. No group contract and no formal peer evaluation.
  • If the task runs for weeks, conditions change, and you need a contract and peer evaluations to keep it fair, you've moved to Sustained — and earned its heavier assessment model.

For Faculty

  • This is the first level with real setup. Blackboard Groups take a few minutes to provision — the settings that matter are covered in 8.0 | Technology Tools — but they give you the group container and the graded record together.
  • Keep the window short. The whole point of Coordinated is interdependence without the long runway where free-riding grows.
  • Require one individual deliverable per person — a part or a contribution note — so a single shared grade isn't the only thing on the line.
  • Dual-credit and adult learners: a one-week task with clear roles, coordinated in Teams chat on students' own time, respects work and caregiving schedules better than requiring a live meeting.
