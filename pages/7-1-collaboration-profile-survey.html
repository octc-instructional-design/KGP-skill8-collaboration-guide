7.1 Collaboration Profile Survey

A Week 1 intake that helps you form workable teams — and gives students an early prompt to think about how they work.

Collect in Week 1  ·  Use before you assign teams

Assigned teams beat self-selected ones, but only if you assign with information — otherwise you've just replaced students' clustering with your own guesswork. These six items give you enough to balance teams on the things that actually break online group work: when people are available, and what they're able to contribute.

Use it for Coordinated and Sustained work. A Quick or Structured activity doesn't need standing teams, so it doesn't need this.

Where to build it. The deciding factor is completion, not features. A survey inside the course, on a page students are already visiting, gets answered; one behind an outside link doesn't. Build it wherever it lives closest to the students — Blackboard's survey tool if you want it in the course flow, Microsoft Forms if you'd rather have its response views and don't mind posting the link in Blackboard. Either way, set responses to be visible only to you, and attach a small number of points or a completion requirement.

Survey Items

  • Name & preferred name
    Short answer.
  • Time zone
    Short answer. Matters for online and dual-credit sections that span time zones.
  • Typical availability for group work
    Multiple choice or checkboxes — e.g., Weekdays 9–5 · Weeknights after 8pm · Weekends only · Varies week to week. Sort on this first. A team that can never overlap will struggle no matter how well its skills complement.
  • Tech comfort level
    Scale of 1–5. Helps you spread confident and less-confident users across teams rather than concentrating them.
  • Preferred role
    Multiple choice — Team Coordinator · Researcher · Writer/Editor · Designer/Presenter. Feeds Indicator 8.3 — distributing roles by strength rather than by who volunteers first.
  • What is one thing you want your teammates to know about your working style?
    Long answer. Usually the most useful item — it surfaces constraints and preferences a checkbox can't, and it's the one students remember writing.

For Faculty

  • Sort by availability first, then balance tech comfort and roles. Scheduling is the constraint that sinks online teams; skill mix is the one you can fix later.
  • Keep responses private. This is intake for grouping, not something to share back to the class.
  • Read the working-style answers individually. They frequently flag something you can design around — caregiving hours, a slow-but-thorough style, an anxiety about presenting.
  • Don't over-engineer the balance. Availability plus one other factor is enough; optimizing across six variables takes an evening and doesn't produce better teams.
  • Reusable every term — duplicate it and update the title.
