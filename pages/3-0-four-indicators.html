The Vocabulary  ·  8.1 to 8.4

The Four Indicators, in Plain Terms

Collaboration is too big to assess as one thing. Split into four, each part becomes something you can actually see a student do — and you only ever need one or two of them for a given assignment.

  • 8.1
    Contributing to Team Goals. Bringing ideas that move the team toward its goal — taking part, not just going along. See Indicator 8.1 · Contributing to Team Goals for “I can” statements and discipline examples.
  • 8.2
    Professional Communication & Feedback. Communicating clearly, responding to others, and handling disagreement like a professional. See Indicator 8.2 · Professional Communication & Feedback.
  • 8.3
    Roles & Responsibilities. Seeing what needs doing, dividing it by strength, and carrying your part. See Indicator 8.3 · Roles & Responsibilities.
  • 8.4
    Adaptability & Trust. Naming your strengths and limits, and adjusting as things change so the team keeps its footing. See Indicator 8.4 · Adaptability & Trust.

Which Indicators Your Activity Can Reach

This is the part that saves you a redesign. Not every indicator is available at every level. A ten-minute discussion can show you 8.1 — it cannot show you 8.3, because nothing was divided. Adaptability needs enough time for something to actually change. Pick the indicator you care about and the level follows, or pick the level and take the indicators it can honestly give you.

Read it both directions. If your course is responsible for 8.3 or 8.4, you need at least a Coordinated activity — a discussion post won't get you there. And if you're working at Quick or Structured, you are building 8.1 and 8.2, which is a complete and legitimate contribution to the skill. Fewer indicators, done honestly, beats four indicators claimed on paper.

The CPE Rubric, Numbered

Essential Skill 8 comes from the Kentucky Graduate Profile (opens in a new tab), the Council on Postsecondary Education's statement of what every Kentucky graduate should be able to do. CPE defines Skill 8 with a single rubric: four indicators of collaboration, each described at three developmental levels — Benchmark (college entry), Milestone (end of two years), and Capstone (bachelor's completion).

That rubric is the authoritative source. To make it usable for course design, this guide does three things with it: it numbers the four indicators 8.1 through 8.4 so they're easy to reference, gives each a short plain-language name, and builds a working card for each that turns the rubric language into “I can” statements, observable behaviors, and discipline examples. The official rubric stays the anchor; the numbering and cards are the working translation.

Indicator Benchmark College Entry Milestone End of Two Years — your target Capstone Bachelor's Completion
8.1 Contributing to Team Goals Supports team decisions by participating in discussions. Contributes ideas aligned to identified objectives of the team. Advocates for evidence-based solutions that promote the team's identified objectives.
8.2 Professional Communication & Feedback Responds promptly to messages and requests for information. Addresses conflicts constructively, communicating expectations and adjusting own behavior based on feedback received. Fosters strong team cohesion by giving and soliciting feedback and responding transparently.
8.3 Roles & Responsibilities Completes tasks on time and recognizes how individual responsibilities impact team goals. Identifies tasks required to reach a goal and learns about team members' assets to support the distribution of roles and responsibilities. Collaborates with multiple teams, utilizing different perspectives and skills to achieve common goals.
8.4 Adaptability & Trust Acknowledges both strengths and limitations within a team. Adapts to changing project requirements and roles in a way that fosters mutual trust and respect amongst team members. Navigates between leading and supporting through advocacy for mutual goals and integration of team members' strengths.
A note on scope. This guide focuses on the Benchmark → Milestone progression — the realistic target for a two-year college, and the reason the Milestone column is the one framed in navy. Capstone (shown in gray) is the bachelor's-completion level; it's here for the full picture and for students who will transfer, but you are not expected to bring students to it in a two-year program.

Rubric language: Kentucky Council on Postsecondary Education, Kentucky Graduate Profile, Essential Skill 8 (rubric dated 9/15/2025). Indicator numbering (8.1–8.4) and short names added for this guide.

How to Use the Indicators

  • You don't need all four at once. Most activities build one or two. Pick the indicator your course is responsible for and design toward it.
  • The numbering is the through-line. Every page in this guide refers to 8.1–8.4 — the levels, the examples, the rubric tool, and the worked exemplars all use these same four.
  • Skim now, return later. Read the four cards once for the shape of the skill, then come back to the specific one when you're designing or grading for it.
