Section 3.3

8.3  Roles & Responsibilities

One of four indicators of Essential Skill 8: “Graduates will collaborate with colleagues, become effective team members, and manage conflict.” This one is about seeing what needs doing, dividing it by strength, and carrying your part.

Assessable from level 3  ·  Coordinated onward

Benchmark · Starting Point Milestone 8.3 · End of Two Years of College
Completes tasks on time and recognizes how individual responsibilities impact team goals. Identifies tasks required to reach a goal and learns about team members' assets to support the distribution of roles and responsibilities.

I Can Statements

  • I can identify what tasks need to be completed for a team to reach its goal.
  • I can recognize what my teammates do well and use that to help distribute work effectively.
  • I can take on a role that fits both the team's needs and my own strengths.

What It Looks Like

Observable behaviors — things you can point at in a real course. Not a checklist to score, and not a count.

  • Participates in breaking a team goal into specific, assignable tasks
  • Identifies at least one strength or asset each teammate brings to the project
  • Accepts and fulfills a role assigned on the basis of team need, not just personal preference
  • Delivers their assigned piece by the date the team agreed on — or tells the team before it's late

How to Assess It

Moves that work in any course. Pick the column that matches where your class meets — the tasks being divided are yours to supply.

In Person

  • Task-and-role board Before any work begins, the team writes out the tasks, who owns each, and a one-line reason. You assess the plan — an early checkpoint, weeks before the product exists.
  • Strengths round Each student names one thing a teammate brings, before roles are assigned. Two minutes, and it makes the “learns about team members' assets” clause real rather than assumed.
  • Handoff check Midway, each member says what they've delivered and what they still owe. Short, public, and it surfaces a stalled role while there's still time.

Online · Asynchronous

  • Team charter submission In a Blackboard Group, the team submits a planning document listing tasks, owners, and dates. Attach points to it, or it won't get done.
  • Separate submissions Each member submits their own piece individually as well as into the team product. Accountability becomes structural instead of something the team has to negotiate.
  • Owner-tagged workspace Every section in the shared file or Teams channel carries its author's name. Role fulfillment is then visible without your having to ask anyone.

Hybrid

  • Assign in the room, record in the course Roles get negotiated face to face, where it's quick, then posted where you can actually grade them.
  • Standing check-in A brief in-class round on what's been delivered, with the written plan updated in the course afterward so the record stays current.

Practical Tips for Faculty

  • Requiring a team planning document at the start makes roles visible and gives you an early assessment point before the project is finished. The group contract in 7.0 | Group Work Tools already does this.
  • Individual accountability is what makes this assessable — design so each member submits something that reflects their specific role. See 5.0 | Design Your Assessment.
  • Peer assessment of role fulfillment is more informative than a general “teamwork” rating — ask peers to assess specific behaviors, not overall impressions
  • If one student could do the whole task alone, this indicator isn't really in play. Real role division is what makes 8.3 visible
