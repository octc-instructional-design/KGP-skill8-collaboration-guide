Section 9.3

Coordinated Collaboration: Idea Bank

Short tasks with genuinely divided labor that build Indicator 8.3 — breaking a goal into parts and distributing roles by strength.

Level 3 · Coordinated  ·  Builds 8.3  ·  Borrow Freely

This is a shorter bank on purpose. Coordinated tasks are bigger and fewer than Quick or Structured ones — the work is genuinely divided and the group can't finish without every part. So instead of twenty small moves, here are five reusable structures and a concrete way to use each, all sized to about a week. The test of a real Coordinated task: if the pieces could be done in any order by anyone, it isn't coordinated — it's parallel work with a shared grade.

To run any of these online: divided work needs a home, so set up a Blackboard Group set; each member posts their part to the group space and the team assembles it in a shared file in their Teams channel. Live coordination happens in a Teams meeting.

Five Interdependent Structures

  • Jigsaw. Split a topic into parts; each member becomes the expert on one and teaches it back; the group assembles the whole. The product needs every piece, so no one can sit out.Interdependence by divided expertise
  • Divided-Role Mini-Project. One short shared deliverable with assigned roles — researcher, builder, checker, presenter. Each owns a section over about a week, and it doesn't come together unless the parts do.Interdependence by divided pieces
  • Reconcile the Evidence. Each member holds a different source, dataset, or observation — and they don't fully agree. The team has to produce one account that addresses the discrepancies rather than averaging them away. The disagreement is the assignment, not a problem with it.Interdependence by built-in conflict
  • Relay Build. A short pipeline where each member's output feeds the next — one drafts, one tests or checks, one documents or presents. Nobody can start until the person before them finishes.Interdependence by sequence
  • Role-Play or Simulation. Assign interdependent roles in a scenario — a team meeting, a case, an incident. Each must play their part for the simulation to function, and the debrief surfaces how the roles depended on one another.Interdependence by divided perspective

These are the same interdependence engines described in Level 4 · Sustained, Part 1 — just sized to a week instead of a term. If you later want a Sustained project, one of these is usually the seed.

One Way to Use Each, by Field

Browsing aids, not boundaries. A job package works as a lab package, an event plan, or a grant packet.

  • Health Sciences — Interprofessional Case Team. A team takes one shared patient case and each member works a different role's angle — nursing, pharmacy, nutrition, social work — contributing their piece to a single combined care plan. No section is complete without the others' input. (Role-play + divided-role.)
  • Technical & Trades — Job Package. A team produces one job package for a project: one owns the materials list, one the safety plan, one the work sequence, one the cost estimate, combined into a single submittable. The package fails if any section is missing. (Divided-role.)
  • Lab Sciences — Conflicting Data Set. Each member is given a different measurement, sample, or reading of the same phenomenon, and the readings don't line up. The team produces one interpretation that accounts for the disagreement and says which evidence it weighted, and why. (Reconcile the evidence.)
  • Business & IT — Client Recommendation. A team delivers one recommendation: one handles research, one the numbers, one the written rationale, one the slide, assembled in a shared Teams file. (IT variant: each owns one system component — network, security, users, backup — and documents the piece the others build on.)
  • General Education & First-Year — Expert Panel. On a multi-part topic, each member becomes the expert on one facet and the group combines the parts into a single briefing, study guide, or short teach-back to the class. The whole only works if every facet shows up. (Jigsaw.)

Making One Yours

  • Keep the window short. A class to about a week. The point of Coordinated is interdependence without the long runway where free-riding grows — that's Sustained's territory.
  • Build in one individual deliverable per person. Their part, or a one-line contribution note, so a single shared grade isn't the only thing on the line.
  • Make the parts truly depend on each other. If a section could be removed without breaking the whole, tighten the task until every role matters. This is the difference between a team task and four assignments in a trench coat.
  • Assign the roles yourself, or by strength. Left to volunteers, the same student takes the writing every time and the same one takes the slide.
  • This is the first level with real setup. Blackboard Groups take a few minutes, and the order matters — see 8.0 | Technology Tools before you build.
