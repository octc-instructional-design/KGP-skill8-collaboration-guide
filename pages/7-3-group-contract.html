Section 7.3

Group Contract

Your team's agreement about how you'll work together.

Complete as a team  ·  Builds Indicators 8.2, 8.3 & 8.4

Setting clear expectations early is the single best way to prevent the problems that derail group projects — uneven workloads, missed messages, and last-minute surprises. Fill this out together in your first team meeting. It only works if everyone has a say and everyone agrees.

1. How we'll communicate 8.2

Our primary channel will be [ Teams / Blackboard / other ]. We agree to check messages and respond within [ 24 / 48 ] hours on school days.

So we know what “quiet” means: if someone hasn't responded in that window, we'll assume something came up and check in — not that they've dropped out.

We've assigned roles based on what each person brings to the team, not just preference. Who is doing what:

3. Deadlines

Internal drafts are due to the group [ 24 / 48 ] hours before the official deadline, so we have time to review before submitting.

4. How we'll make decisions

If we disagree, we will [ talk it through and vote / bring it to the instructor / other: __________ ]. Disagreeing about the work is normal and expected — we'll aim to settle it ourselves before escalating.

5. When something changes 8.4

Things come up — work shifts, illness, family, a class that suddenly gets heavy. We agree to tell the team before it affects our part, not after. When that happens, the team will decide together what gets covered and note what changed, so the work is redistributed openly rather than quietly absorbed by whoever notices first.

6. Accountability & conflict 8.2

If a member misses a deadline or goes quiet, we will reach out to them once. If we don't hear back within [ 24 / 48 ] hours, we will let the instructor know. We agree to raise concerns directly and professionally rather than letting them build up.

It is not the team's job to enforce participation. Reach out once, then tell the instructor — that's the whole responsibility.

For Faculty

  • Collect it by Week 2 (or your first project milestone) — it's your earliest assessment point for Indicator 8.3, weeks before any product exists.
  • Item 1 is doing more work than it looks like. An agreed response window turns “quiet” from a feeling into something measurable — for the team and for you.
  • Item 6 is the one to say out loud. Students can't discipline each other, and asking them to try is what produces the resentment they bring to the next group project.
  • A signed contract gives you something concrete to point back to if a conflict surfaces later — you're referring to their agreement, not imposing yours.
  • Have teams assign roles by strength using the Collaboration Profile Survey, not by who volunteers first.
