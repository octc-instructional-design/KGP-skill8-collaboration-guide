Section 9.4

Geologic Site Investigation

A fully online GLY 111 team project where each member analyzes a different line of geologic evidence and the team must reconcile conflicting observations into one interpretation.

Level 4 · Sustained  ·  Online Geology Lab  ·  Surfaces all four indicators

Alignment. Targets the Benchmark → Milestone progression. Built for a fully online, one-credit lab, so it runs leaner than a full-term project — about six weeks, four-person teams, working in Blackboard Groups and a Microsoft Teams channel. It serves the GLY 111 outcomes directly: collecting and analyzing data, evaluating data and reconciling conflicting observations, interpreting results, identifying minerals and rocks, and using maps. That reconciliation outcome is the hinge — it is a collaboration-and-conflict task by definition, which is exactly where Skill 8 lives.

Why This Needs a Team — and Works Online

A real geologic interpretation pulls together mineral identification, rock analysis, landform and map reading, and water or hazard data — more than one student can do well in the time a one-credit lab allows. And the evidence genuinely conflicts: a landform suggests one history, a rock sample suggests another, and the team has to reason to a defensible interpretation. That's interdependence by built-in conflict — the strongest of the engines in Level 4 · Sustained, because the disagreement is in the material rather than manufactured by the assignment.

It's also how geoscientists actually work, which makes the online setting an advantage rather than a workaround: distributed teammates pooling and integrating data is the authentic professional practice. Real interdependence, sustained effort, reconciliation of conflict, and reflection together give the project high-impact-practice fidelity.

The Scenario

Your team is a small geological survey unit assigned to investigate one site — for example, the karst landscape around Mammoth Cave, the Kentucky River Palisades, or a coal-bearing area of eastern Kentucky. Using your lab kit specimens, topographic and geologic maps, and Google Earth, each of you investigates a different line of evidence. Your observations will not all point the same direction. Your job is to reconcile them into one coherent interpretation of the site — what it is made of, how it formed, and what hazards or resources it holds — plus a short memo on how your team resolved the disagreements.

Faculty note: assign a site with enough variety that the evidence lines genuinely interact, and keep it real enough that accuracy matters. Kentucky locations make maps and Google Earth immediately usable.

Roles

Each member owns one line of evidence, so no interpretation is complete without the others. Assign by the strengths surfaced in the Week 1 survey.

  • Mineralogist — identifies and analyzes the minerals in the kit specimens (cleavage, hardness, streak), and vouches for sample accuracy.
  • Petrologist — identifies and classifies the rocks (igneous, sedimentary, metamorphic) and links them to formation processes.
  • Map & Landform Analyst — reads topographic and geologic maps and Google Earth for landforms, structure, and drainage.
  • Data Lead & Team Coordinator — compiles measurements, checks accuracy and precision, leads the reconciliation of conflicting evidence, handles water and hazard data, and submits to Blackboard.

Online logistics: each member's data lives in the team's Teams channel; the Week 4 reconciliation runs as a Teams meeting, or asynchronously in the channel if schedules don't align. The Blackboard Group set holds the final submission and the attached rubric — build the group set before the assignment, per 8.0 | Technology Tools.

Week 4 is also where you'll be tempted to step in. Don't, while the argument is about the evidence — that disagreement is the assignment. Step in only if it turns personal or a voice stops appearing. See 2.0 | Facilitating Effective Group Work.

Student Instructions (copy into the assignment)

  1. Meet your team and set the rules. In your Teams channel, introduce yourselves and complete the Group Contract. Agree on how often you'll check in and what you'll do if someone goes quiet.
  2. Claim your evidence. Take one role — minerals, rocks, maps, or data — based on strengths, not who answers first.
  3. Collect and post. Analyze your line of evidence and post your findings to the channel so the team can see all four together.
  4. Reconcile. Compare findings, name exactly where they conflict, and reason together to one defensible interpretation. This is the core of the work — disagreement is expected; how you handle it is the point.
  5. Build the deliverables. Co-write the integrated interpretation and the Reconciliation Memo explaining how you resolved the conflicts.
  6. Submit. Your Data Lead uploads the deliverables to Blackboard by the deadline. Teams is where you work; Blackboard is where it counts.
  7. Reflect. Complete your final peer evaluation and individual reflection.

Reflection Prompt (individual, ~250 words)

Reread your Week 1 Disagreement Self-Check first — especially what you said you find hard and what you wanted to practice. Then describe a moment when your line of evidence conflicted with a teammate's. How did the team work through it, and what would you do differently next time? Close by connecting it: which part of “collaborate, be an effective team member, and manage conflict” did reconciling that disagreement actually grow in you, and where are you still developing?

Adapting This for Your Lab

  • Swap the site and the evidence lines. Any lab science where data comes from multiple sources and can conflict fits this shape — biology, environmental science, chemistry.
  • The reconciliation is the engine. Keep an outcome that forces the team to resolve genuine disagreement; that's what makes it Skill 8 rather than four separate worksheets.
  • Make sure the evidence really can conflict. If every line points the same way, the team has nothing to reconcile and you're back to parallel work with a shared grade.
  • Build your own product rubric from the indicators using 6.0 | Build Your Rubric; the process rubric above transfers unchanged.
