Section 3.4

8.4  Adaptability & Trust

One of four indicators of Essential Skill 8: “Graduates will collaborate with colleagues, become effective team members, and manage conflict.” This one is about naming your strengths and limits, and adjusting as things change so the team keeps its footing.

Assessable at level 4  ·  Sustained (partly at level 3) — things need time to change

Benchmark · Starting Point Milestone 8.4 · End of Two Years of College
Acknowledges both strengths and limitations within a team. Adapts to changing project requirements and roles in a way that fosters mutual trust and respect amongst team members.

I Can Statements

  • I can adjust my role or approach when project needs change without creating disruption for the team.
  • I can respond to unexpected changes in a way that keeps the team's trust and momentum intact.
  • I can recognize when flexibility is needed and act on it professionally.

What It Looks Like

Observable behaviors — things you can point at in a real course. Not a checklist to score, and not a count.

  • Adjusts their approach or role when project requirements shift, rather than continuing with the original plan
  • Tells the team when their own circumstances will affect the work — before it affects the work
  • Takes on a task outside their original role when the team needs it covered
  • Keeps working with teammates after a disagreement rather than withdrawing or going around them

How to Assess It

Moves that work in any course. Note that all of them require a project long enough for something to actually change — the change is the assessment.

In Person

  • Planned disruption Midway, change one thing — the scope, a constraint, a task whose owner is absent. Observe who re-plans and who carries on with the original plan as if nothing happened.
  • Role swap Partway through, two members trade responsibilities. What you're assessing is the handoff: what gets explained, and whether the team keeps its footing.
  • Name the adjustment After the disruption, each student says in one sentence what they changed. Fast, verbal, and it separates adapting from simply absorbing.

Online · Asynchronous

  • Revise the requirements in writing Post a changed specification mid-project. The response thread is your evidence — who acknowledges it, who re-plans, who never mentions it.
  • Early-warning norm The contract requires members to post when their circumstances will affect the work, before it does. Whether anyone used it is directly visible in the record.
  • Coverage log When someone can't deliver, the team records who covered what. Shows adaptation, and it stops silent absorption — which is where the resentment comes from.

Hybrid

  • Announce live, re-plan in the course The room absorbs the surprise, where reactions are quick and visible; the tool captures the revised plan, where it's assessable.
  • Closing reflection Each student writes on one moment they had to adapt — what changed, how they responded, what they'd do differently. Catches what observation misses.

Practical Tips for Faculty

  • This indicator needs a project long enough for something to actually change. If your activity runs a week or less, 8.4 isn't available — and that's fine, because you're building 8.1 and 8.2 instead
  • Introducing a deliberate disruption is one of the most authentic ways to assess adaptability — a simple change in scope or timeline works
  • Proactive communication when things shift is a professional skill — make it explicit that students are expected to notify teammates when their circumstances change, and assess it. An agreed response window in the group contract gives this a definition.
  • End-of-project individual reflections give you insight into adaptability that peer reviews and observation alone may miss
