4.0 Choosing Your Approach

Not all teamwork is a group project. Here is the full range of ways to build the skill — and a direct method for picking the right-sized one for your course.

Four Levels  ·  Quick to Sustained

The point of this page: you do not need a group project to teach Skill 8. Collaboration runs on a scale — from a two-minute exchange in class to a sustained multi-week team project. What grows along that scale is one thing: how much there is to coordinate, and for how long. The larger the activity, the more interdependence, the more time, the more logistics — and the more of the four indicators it can reach. A course can fully satisfy its Skill 8 obligation with Quick and Structured activities and never run anything that looks like a “group project.”

The Scale at a Glance

Read it two ways: left to right to see what each level builds, or start from the indicator you need and read off the smallest level that reaches it. Each level includes the ones before it — a Coordinated activity builds 8.1, 8.2, and 8.3.

The Four Levels

Level 1 · Quick

A quick collaborative move

What it builds: 8.1 — Contributing to team goals. One step, one class session, no standing team, no group grade. Students share ideas toward a shared question; that is already Skill 8.

  • Think-pair-share before a concept is formalized
  • A one-minute paper passed to a neighbor for one addition
  • A five-minute small-group problem, then report out

Assessment fit: folds into participation. Nothing to build in Blackboard, no rubric, no contract. An optional one-line reflection is plenty.

Level 2 · Structured

A structured exchange

What it builds: 8.2 — Professional communication & feedback (8.1 still present). A protocol and a few moving parts, but still short — a class or a few days. Students respond to and build on each other's work, and the first real friction shows up.

  • Structured peer review of individual work, using a protocol
  • Fishbowl or structured discussion with response requirements
  • Paired critique where each must give one specific, actionable note

Assessment fit: the feedback a student writes is the evidence — grade the quality of the feedback, not just that it happened. Light individual grade. Still no group grade, no contract.

Level 3 · Coordinated

A coordinated short task

What it builds: 8.3 — Roles & responsibilities (plus 8.1, 8.2). Now the work is genuinely divided and the group can't succeed without every part — real dependencies between people — but the timeline is short enough that free-riding has little room to develop.

  • Jigsaw — each person owns a piece the group needs
  • A one-week paired or small-group analysis with a single shared deliverable
  • A role-play or simulation with assigned, interdependent roles

Assessment fit: a light shared-deliverable grade plus a quick individual accountability piece — each submits their part or a short contribution note. A one-question peer check is optional. Not the full three-part model in 5.0.

Level 4 · Sustained

A sustained team project

What it builds: 8.4 — Adaptability & trust (all four present). The only level that reliably reaches 8.4, because adaptation and sustained trust need time to pass and conditions to change. You cannot see 8.4 in a 50-minute activity — there is nothing yet to adapt to.

  • A multi-week group project — see the worked exemplars in 9.0 | Examples, including COM 252 and GLY 111
  • A semester-long team or capstone

Assessment fit: the full apparatus earns its keep here — the three-part grading model in 5.0, a group contract, scaffolded milestones, and midpoint plus final peer evaluations. Only choose a sustained project if you specifically need 8.4. Don't reach for a project to assess 8.1 or 8.2; a smaller level does that better, with far less to manage.

How to Choose — Five Steps

  • Name the indicator you owe. Decide which of 8.1–8.4 your course is responsible for, or which one you want to build right now. The reach table in 3.0 shows what's available where.
  • Read the size it needs. 8.1 needs only a Quick move. 8.2 needs a Structured exchange. 8.3 needs Coordinated, divided work. 8.4 needs a Sustained project. That is the smallest level that reaches the indicator.
  • Pick that level — or a larger one, if you also want the indicators below it. Bigger is not better; it just costs more to run.
  • Match the assessment to the size. Don't bolt a contract and a peer evaluation onto a think-pair-share. A Quick move folds into participation; only a Sustained project earns the full model.
  • Don't scale up unless you need 8.4. If your obligation is 8.1 and 8.2, three small structured activities across the term meet it completely — with no group project at all.

The level you pick also sets what it costs you to run once it's live — roughly five minutes total at Quick, about fifteen minutes a week at Sustained. The full breakdown is in 2.0 | Facilitating Effective Group Work.

Three well-run Quick and Structured activities across a term is a legitimate, complete way to teach Essential Skill 8. The group project is one level on the scale — the largest one — not the definition of the skill.

A Note on Modality

The level pages that follow show each activity three ways: in person, online synchronous, and online asynchronous. Hybrid isn't a separate mode — it's a routing decision you make piece by piece, choosing whether each part of the activity happens in the room or in the tool. The rule that holds up: anything that produces evidence goes in the tool; anything that needs speed or repair happens in the room. That's covered in 2.0, and the indicator cards in 3.1–3.4 show a hybrid version of each assessment move.

For Faculty

  • Most courses live in the first two levels. Quick and Structured fit almost any course, online or in person, with no group logistics to manage.
  • The indicators don't all need the same size. 8.1 and 8.2 are reachable almost anywhere; 8.3 needs real coordination; 8.4 genuinely needs duration. Match the level to the indicator, not to a default idea of “group work.”
  • Smaller is often the honest choice. If a project would mostly generate logistics and free-rider complaints without reaching 8.4, a Coordinated or Structured activity assesses the same indicators more cleanly.
  • Each level includes the ones before it. Choosing a larger level to capture a lower indicator works, but you pay for it in time and administration — spend that only when you want what the top of the scale offers.
