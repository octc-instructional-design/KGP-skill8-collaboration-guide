7.4 Peer Evaluation

A confidential rating of each teammate's collaboration, built on the four indicators.

Use at the midpoint and at the end

This rates the behaviors that make a team work, drawn straight from the four indicators — not a vague overall “teamwork” impression. Run it once at the midpoint, so the feedback still has time to change something, and once at the end. Students complete one set of ratings for each teammate, plus a self-rating.

Confidentiality. Individual responses are visible only to the instructor. Tell students that before they start — they'll rate more honestly, and it makes clear this is about helping the team work, not settling scores. Build it wherever you can guarantee that: a form linked from the course works, as long as the responses don't come back to the team.

For Faculty

  • This feeds the Process part (20–30%) of the three-part model in 5.0 — an individual grade, not a shared one.
  • Don't use peer scores as a multiplier on the group grade. It converts a rough impression into a precise-looking number, and it hands students power over each other's grades that they didn't ask for. Use the ratings as evidence you weigh, alongside what you can see yourself.
  • The written answers are worth more than the ratings. “Sometimes” tells you nothing; one specific sentence tells you what happened.
  • Watch for a large gap between a student's self-rating and their peers' — it's a useful, low-drama opening for a conversation.
  • The midpoint round is the one that changes outcomes. A single end-of-term evaluation only documents what already happened.
  • Every item names a behavior, so a low rating points to something you can coach rather than a personality you can't.
