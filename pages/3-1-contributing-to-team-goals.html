Section 3.1

8.1  Contributing to Team Goals

One of four indicators of Essential Skill 8: “Graduates will collaborate with colleagues, become effective team members, and manage conflict.” This one is about bringing ideas that move the team toward its goal — taking part, not just going along.

Assessable at every level  ·  1 Quick through 4 Sustained

Benchmark · Starting Point Milestone 8.1 · End of Two Years of College
Supports team decisions by participating in discussions. Contributes ideas aligned to identified objectives of the team.

I Can Statements

  • I can share ideas that are relevant to what my team is trying to accomplish.
  • I can participate in team discussions in a way that moves the group toward its goal.
  • I can connect my contributions to the team's objectives rather than just my own perspective.

What It Looks Like

Observable behaviors — things you can point at in a real course. Not a checklist to score, and not a count.

  • Offers at least one idea or suggestion during team discussions that directly relates to the team's stated goal
  • Explains how their contribution connects to the team's objectives when asked
  • Weighs in when the team makes a decision rather than going along with whatever is proposed first
  • Builds on others' ideas rather than redirecting the conversation away from team goals

How to Assess It

Moves that work in any course. Pick the column that matches where your class meets — the content of the discussion is yours to supply.

In Person

  • Observation checklist During team planning, note whether each student offers at least one idea tied to the stated goal. A tally is enough — you're recording that a contribution happened, not judging its quality.
  • Contribute before proceeding Structure the task so the team can't move to the next step until every member has put an idea on the table. Makes 8.1 automatic to observe.
  • Exit slip Two lines from each student: what they contributed, and how it connected to the team's goal. Collected at the door.

Online · Asynchronous

  • Post, then reply Each student posts one idea tied to the team's objective, then builds on a classmate's. The thread is your evidence — timestamped and attributable, with nothing for you to witness live.
  • Contribution before consensus Require every member to post before the team converges on one direction. Prevents the first-poster-wins pattern that makes later contributions look like agreement.
  • Named planning document In a Blackboard Group, the team drafts a shared plan in which each contribution carries its author's name.

Hybrid

  • Discuss live, commit in writing Generate ideas in the room, then have each student post their own contribution to the course. The room gets the energy; the tool gets the record.
  • Flip it Students post ideas before class and spend the session reconciling them. Everyone arrives having already contributed, including the ones who don't speak up in a room.

Practical Tips for Faculty

  • Make team objectives explicit and visible before any group activity — students can't contribute toward a goal they haven't clearly identified
  • A simple contribution log (who said what, how it connected to the goal) gives you something concrete to assess during or after group work
  • Peer assessment is one of the most efficient ways to surface whether contributions were meaningful — build it into the assignment, not as an afterthought. The peer evaluation form is in 7.0 | Group Work Tools.
  • In online courses, threaded discussions make individual contributions visible in a way that in-person group work often doesn't — which makes 8.1 easier to assess online, not harder
